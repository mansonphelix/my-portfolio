VLAN Configuration for Office Networks

Networking · 13 min read

What you'll learn

  • How to plan VLANs for office, guest and CCTV traffic
  • Switch port assignment and trunk configuration
  • Inter-VLAN routing on routers and Layer 3 switches
  • Common VLAN pitfalls and how to avoid them

VLANs help separate traffic types on a shared network. In an office, you might isolate management systems, staff devices, guest Wi-Fi and CCTV onto different VLANs. This reduces broadcast traffic and makes troubleshooting much simpler.

1. Plan your VLANs

  • VLAN 10: Staff devices
  • VLAN 20: Servers and management
  • VLAN 30: Guest Wi-Fi
  • VLAN 40: CCTV and security

2. Configure the switch

Create each VLAN on the switch and assign access ports to the appropriate VLAN. Keep a spreadsheet of port-to-VLAN mappings for easy audits.

3. Configure the router or Layer 3 switch

If your devices on different VLANs need to communicate, configure inter-VLAN routing. On a router this is typically done with sub-interfaces; on a managed Layer 3 switch it is often simpler.

4. Test and verify

  • Confirm devices can reach their default gateway
  • Verify that a device on one VLAN cannot reach another VLAN unless routed
  • Check DHCP scope and DNS resolution

5. Common pitfalls

  • Forgetting native VLAN settings on trunk links
  • Mismatched VLAN IDs between switches
  • Allowing all VLANs on a trunk when only a subset is needed
