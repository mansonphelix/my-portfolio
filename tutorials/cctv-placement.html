CCTV Camera Placement & Best Practices

CCTV · 9 min read

What you'll learn

  • Identifying key surveillance areas and blind spots
  • Camera angles, height and mounting considerations
  • Lighting conditions and IR camera selection
  • NVR placement, recording settings and cable labelling

A surveillance system is only as good as its coverage. Poor camera placement wastes time during playback and can leave blind spots. This checklist is based on real deployments for offices and municipal buildings.

1. Identify key areas

  • Entry and exit points
  • Reception, waiting areas and cash-handling zones
  • Server rooms, storerooms and restricted areas
  • Perimeter fences and external car parks

2. Camera angles and height

Mount cameras 2.5m to 3.5m high where possible. Angle them to capture faces and vehicle plates without being obvious targets for tampering. Avoid pointing cameras directly at bright light sources.

3. Lighting conditions

  • Use IR cameras for areas with no night lighting
  • Supplement dark corners with low-wattage external lights
  • Check for backlighting from windows or headlights
  • Test night vision before finalising camera positions

4. Recording settings

Set continuous recording for critical areas and motion-based recording for low-traffic zones. Use a bitrate and resolution that balances storage with the ability to identify people or plates during playback.

5. NVR placement and security

Store the NVR in a locked room or cabinet. Keep it on UPS backup so recordings continue during short power cuts. Label all cables at both ends for easier maintenance.
